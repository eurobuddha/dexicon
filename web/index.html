Three protocols. Together, Dexicon.

Dexicon joins three open protocols on Minima: cross-chain swaps to bring dollars in, liquidity pools anyone can earn from, and an exchange where the two meet. The Dexicon client for Android speaks all three. Anyone is free to build another.

1. MxUSD

Bring dollars in. The AtomiX atomic-swap protocol: swap USDT on Ethereum for MxUSD or MINIMA, peer to peer, across two chains.

  • Atomic swaps with hash time-locked contracts. Either both sides pay, or both are refunded.
  • USDT to MxUSD, or USDT to MINIMA, one market at a time.
  • Make a market, or trade OTC with a known counterparty.
  • A built-in Ethereum wallet derived from your node.

2. Pools

Add liquidity and earn, or swap. The PandaPools protocol: anyone can put MINIMA and MxUSD into their own pool and earn 0.5% on every swap that passes through it.

  • Your liquidity stays in your own pool, at your address. Add more or withdraw any time.
  • Swap straight against the pools, routed across all of them.
  • A verified backup is required before a pool goes live.
  • Recovery from the chain's archive if a device is lost.

3. Exchange

Trade on the book. The Dexicon order protocol: fast off-chain limit orders and every pool's liquidity in one ladder, so there is always a market.

  • One-tap takes, or take a percentage of an order.
  • A market-making ladder that can follow the MEXC price.
  • Your orders stay live in the background, even after a reboot.
  • Pool liquidity fills the book, so it's never empty while a pool exists.

1. AtomiX: dollars in, across chains.

AtomiX swaps USDT on Ethereum for MxUSD or MINIMA on Minima, directly with another person. No bridge, no exchange and no wrapped token in between: each side's coins wait in a time-locked contract on its own chain until both sides can be paid.

One secret, two chains.

Both locks open with the same secret. The moment it's used to claim on one chain, it's public, and the other side can claim with it on the other.

All or nothing.

Each lock has a deadline. If anyone stops halfway, the deadlines pass and every coin goes back to where it came from. Nobody can end up with both sides.

Makers, not middlemen.

Anyone can make a market: publish a price for USDT to MxUSD or USDT to MINIMA, and your node completes the swaps it accepts on its own. Or agree a trade privately, over the counter.

  1. Pick a price.Choose a maker's offer, or agree one privately.
  2. Lock with a secret.The side that starts the swap creates a secret and locks first, against its fingerprint.
  3. The other side locks.The counterparty locks on the other chain against the same fingerprint, with an earlier deadline.
  4. Claim.The starter claims the counterparty's coins, which reveals the secret.
  5. Claim back.The counterparty uses the revealed secret to claim the starter's coins. Done.

Your Ethereum wallet comes with your node. The client derives it from your Minima node, so there is nothing extra to set up. Only the Ethereum side pays gas, to Ethereum.

2. PandaPools: a pool of pools.

If you know Uniswap, you know the idea: add liquidity to a pool, and earn a share of every swap it makes. PandaPools does it on Minima's UTXO chain, where every pool belongs to the person who funded it, and the 0.5% goes straight to them.

Your pool is two coins.

A pool is a MINIMA coin and an MxUSD coin, locked under a contract that only lets them trade along the curve. They stay at your address, under your key. There is no shared vault holding everyone's money.

Every pool, one swap.

Every pool is owned by one person. A swap is split across all of them at once, best price first, in a single transaction that either completes fully or not at all.

Add liquidity. Earn 0.5%.

Put MINIMA and MxUSD into your own pool and every swap through it pays you 0.5%. The yield accrues inside your pool, so it grows as people trade. No LP tokens, no protocol cut, and you can add more or take it all back whenever you choose.

3. The exchange: always a market.

Off-chain where speed matters, on-chain where trust matters. Fast limit orders move peer to peer in milliseconds, every pool adds its liquidity to the same ladder, and money only ever moves on chain.

Fast limit orders.

Signed off chain, shared across the network instantly, and settled wallet to wallet in one Minima block. Placing or cancelling an order costs nothing.

Never an empty book.

Every pool is quoted as orders at every price along its curve. As long as a single pool exists, there is always something to trade against.

One ladder, best price.

Fast orders, on-chain orders and pool liquidity sit side by side, each level showing where its depth comes from. A take fills from whichever source is best.

One fast trade, start to finish.

  1. 1Sign
  2. 2Share
  3. 3Take
  4. 4Co-sign
  5. 5Settle

Money moves once, in step 5, and the trade is final after one Minima block.

1. You sign an order.

Pick a price and an amount. Your client writes an order and signs it with a trading key kept on your device, separate from your wallet's keys. The order names the exact coins in your wallet that back it.

Nothing leaves your wallet. An order is a promise, not a deposit.

2. The network shares it.

Your order spreads across a federated network of nodes, powered by Minima and Maxima and run by the people who use them. Each node checks the signature and passes the order on unchanged. Orders and sealed messages travel this way; funds never do.

Anyone running a Parlons node takes part. No single node is special, and nodes can come and go while trading carries on.

3. Someone takes it.

A buyer takes your order. Their client builds one Minima transaction: their coins to you, your coins to them. They sign their half and send it to you in a sealed message only your node can open.

4. Your node co-signs.

Your client checks the transaction against your order: right price, right amount, nothing else touched. Then it adds your signature. It runs in the background, even with the screen off.

5. The chain settles it.

The finished transaction goes to the Minima network and settles in one block. Your order republishes with whatever is left.

Alongside the fast orders, the same ladder carries liquidity that lives entirely on chain: resting on-chain orders and every PandaPools pool.

Join in three steps.

To use the protocol you need your own Minima node and a Dexicon client. Today's client runs on Android phones and tablets. If you're new, give yourself half an hour, a charged device and Wi-Fi for the first sync.

1. Get a Bear.

A Bear is a Minima node and wallet for an Android phone or tablet. It's your account on the protocol. Android 9 or newer.

Install it from a store that checks every download, or straight from its release.
